# Agentic Journey

Our agent's evaluation scores were improving while the people using it were telling us the answers were getting worse. We had built a system to help us recognize failure and it was giving us confidence at exactly the moment we needed to question it. The evidence that eventually changed our understanding came from colleagues who knew enough about the work to recognize that something was wrong.

Their messages carried something our evaluator couldn't see. They understood what people were trying to accomplish and why an answer that passed a test could still fail them. Listening to those messages changed where we looked and what we did next. It also raised a question that reaches well beyond software. How does an organization recognize what it needs to learn?

*Agentic Journey* follows our work at momoGood from a single agent built to help investigate messaging incidents to systems through which people and agents search for evidence and work through problems together. Each new capability gave us another way to act. Each failure revealed something about the knowledge and judgment those actions depended on. Along the way we began to see how building agents could change the way an organization learns.

The idea connecting these experiences is **operational context**. Knowing what to do depends on understanding the circumstances around the work. An error means more when we can trace it through the code to the decision behind it. A question changes when someone explains what they're trying to achieve. The context we can discover and share shapes what receives our attention and which actions become possible.

Acting gives us something further to learn from. An investigation uncovers a missing relationship. A colleague challenges an assumption. A result changes our understanding of the problem. Building learning systems means giving those discoveries somewhere to go so they can improve the next investigation and inform the next decision. It also means creating an organization where people are willing to contribute what they know and question what appears to be working.

This book follows that possibility through the systems we built and the people who helped us understand them. It asks what becomes possible when the experience gained from doing the work can strengthen the organization's capacity to do what comes next.
