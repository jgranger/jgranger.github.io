We live in a messaging based world. If we look closely we can see messaging all around us. In our work, in our homes, in how we relate with other people and organizations. Humans exchange spoken words, written words, gestures, facial expressions and even silence at times. Computers exchange network packets, API requests, events, function calls, and signals. As humans we're much more than AI agents, but there are striking simularities. We have intelligence, autonomy, and agency as we interact with others to accomplish tasks. We're constantly processing messages and have to correlate them with the world around us in relation to other events happening in real time. It's a lot of processing, so no wonder we get tired sometimes! We're processing hundreds, even thousands of messages per minute. We're constantly scanning... looking for feedback, both positive and negative to make sense of this world.

Now imagine doing that for millions of messages every day with an acceptable failure rate of zero. This is the world we live in at momoGood, and the reason we built our first Mastra agent. Initially, the sole purpose was to assist in responding to production messaging events and provide actionable guidance based on historical patterns of behavior. We already had an incredibly reliable messaging system. We even had automation to assist with specific types of messaging events when something unexpected happened. In the rare case a message slipped through our defenses the agent showed up for us. Showing up when it really matters is everything in this business, and when the agent started showing up reliably we knew we were on to something.

Once we started interacting with the agent, we soon wanted more. We wanted it to search our codebase, analyze code snippets and classes, create tickets, and search our API to return message and subscriber data. The messaging agent was becoming much more than something that responds to messaging events, it was like a little Swiss Army Knife we always had in our pocket. We could even throw screenshots of dashboard data at it for quick analysis, which it could correlate to momoGood specific messaging data. It was a good little helper.

It's pretty easy to see the messaging based nature of the architecture above. Regardless of your role in an organization you can probably grasp what's going on pretty quickly. If you're a developer, the above example may even seem a bit boring or trivial but I would challenge you to consider that messaging exists at layers we sometimes don't even consider. Ask a hundred developers about object oriented programming, and most will provide some explanation about classes, inheritance, encapsulation...maybe even polymorphism. You can count on one hand those who will talk about message passing, which is how all of those concepts actually work under the hood.

Message passing describes how the program comes alive. It isn't just a programming technique. It's a communication pattern that repeats at every level of computing.

People provide solutions through creative problem solving and the primary way we do so is by passing messages to each other in pursuit of greater good. What we're modeling with agents at momoGood is a reflection of a repeating pattern that has existed throughout human history. Communication with intelligent beings inherintely demands message passing and to be good at it context is key. In the next few chapters I share more insight into just how important context really is, and more importantly how to properly construct and navigate it. Futher into the book I share our experience building agents, rolling out an agentic platform, what we learned, and how the introduction of agents is helping to transform our business.

Regardless of your role in an organization I think you will benefit from reading about our journey. There's a little something for everyone, and I welcome you to learn from our experiences. If you're interested in using AI to get better, to innovate, to solve problems - this book is for you.  Perhaps you're interested in how AI is shaping organizational culture, well there's something in here for you as well. 

In subsequent chapters I propose something more meaningful. In the age of AI human interaction will continue to be the most powerful form of message passing. If you don't believe me consider that a billion dollar deal can rest on one message alone.

The world is full of problems and most of these problems can be solved through more effective communication and interaction. Join me as I share our journey at momoGood.
